\section{Introduction}
\label{intro}

Natural disasters such as earthquakes, floods, and cyclones threaten human life, residential infrastructure, and community resilience. Existing disaster-management systems use public monitoring, wireless sensing, predictive modelling, and emergency-response mechanisms to identify hazards and support timely decisions \cite{zeeshan2025flood, meenakshi2023wireless}. However, regional warnings generally provide similar information to all households and do not adequately account for building characteristics, household composition, hazard distance, or individual vulnerability. Studies on household resilience show that demographic and socioeconomic conditions significantly influence disaster preparedness and response \cite{fahad2023household}.

A personalized disaster-warning system must combine hazard information with household-specific context. Machine-learning models can estimate physical building damage from structural and hazard-related features, but damage prediction alone cannot provide understandable safety instructions or coordinate communication. Recent research has explored predictive AI and multi-agent systems for household hazard prevention and resilient disaster response \cite{homeguardian2024predictive, aththanayake2026resqconnect}. Similarly, Large Language Models can generate emergency instructions, but unrestricted generation may produce unsupported structural claims, unsafe medical advice, or fabricated evacuation information. Retrieval-Augmented Generation and grounded LLM frameworks are therefore useful for disaster early-warning and emergency-response applications \cite{nugroho2025rag, xie2025deepseek, cui2026llmagents}.

To address these limitations, this paper presents \textit{SentinelHome}, a personalized multi-hazard disaster early-warning and household-response system for earthquakes, floods, and cyclones. The system combines public hazard monitoring, household registration, building-profile analysis, machine-learning-based damage prediction, household vulnerability scoring, document-grounded safety guidance, and alert coordination. Earthquake monitoring and building-damage classification currently form the most complete operational component, while flood and cyclone support are represented through extensible hazard categories and the disaster-safety document corpus.

SentinelHome assigns separate responsibilities to its computational components. The XGBoost model estimates physical building damage using structural and earthquake-related features. A backend personalization layer combines physical damage risk with hazard severity and household vulnerability, including household size and vulnerable-member information, to calculate the final urgency score. The RAG component retrieves safety guidance from curated earthquake, flood, cyclone, and general-preparedness documents. Gemini converts the structured assessment and retrieved evidence into concise instructions while preserving source traceability. LangGraph coordinates assessment, silent termination, advice generation, alerting, confirmation observation, and escalation. This separation prevents the LLM from independently calculating or replacing the ML-based physical-risk prediction.

The research question of this work is:

\textit{\textbf{How can a multi-hazard disaster-warning system combine public hazard monitoring, building-damage prediction, household vulnerability, and retrieved safety guidance to produce personalized and actionable emergency communication?}}

The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}

\item A household-centred multi-hazard monitoring architecture for earthquakes, floods, and cyclones, with earthquake monitoring as the current operational path.

\item A USGS-based earthquake-normalization pipeline using coordinate validation, Haversine distance calculation, hypocentral-distance derivation, event-signature tracking, and household-level hazard filtering.

\item An XGBoost building-damage classification pipeline trained on 1,599,952 records, with the reported accuracy and weighted F$_1$ performance from the training notebook.

\item An LLM-assisted parameter-generation and validation layer that derives the model input contract from API data, registered household information, approximations, and controlled defaults.

\item A page-aware RAG pipeline using LangChain, Voyage AI embeddings, Pinecone, hazard filtering, and Maximum Marginal Relevance retrieval, followed by grounded Gemini guidance generation.

\item A LangGraph response workflow that connects assessment, advice, alert persistence, confirmation, and escalation while preserving traceable state and errors.

\item A Flask and React/Vite full-stack system with contact management, MongoDB-compatible persistence, Telegram alert delivery, Twilio notification boundaries, household confirmation, and simulation isolation.

\end{itemize}

The current implementation includes household registration, primary and relative contact management, earthquake fetching, geospatial filtering, runtime LLM parameter generation, parameter validation, XGBoost inference, personalized urgency scoring, page-aware PDF processing, Pinecone-based RAG retrieval, grounded Gemini guidance, LangGraph execution, alert persistence, Telegram delivery, confirmation handling, and configured voice-escalation support. The protected simulation console exercises the same intelligence pipeline using isolated simulation state so that demonstration data does not modify live household monitoring.

Production authentication and authorization, inbound Telegram confirmation replies, live flood and cyclone damage models, shelter or route databases, and complete background-job hardening remain future work. Automatic voice escalation also depends on valid Twilio configuration and provider availability.
